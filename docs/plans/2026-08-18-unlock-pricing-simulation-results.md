# Simulation results — class-unlock pricing and combat abuse

Companion evidence for `2026-08-17-cross-class-unlock-pricing-cold-review.md`. Generated by
`testing/scripts/sim-combat-abuse.mjs` (engine v0.350), which builds the strongest combat package an
origin class can reach inside a level's AP budget, shopping in any class, under each candidate unlock
model. Score is the sum of engine `tier` over an explicitly named 37-feature combat pool; pool maximum
is **139**.

> **Budgets here are net.** A character's AP budget also pays for Hit Dice and proficiency bonus, which
> at level 20 alone cost **162 of the 535**. An earlier run compared feature spend against the raw
> budget and concluded every class could buy the whole pool with AP to spare — false, and the error
> grew with level, which manufactured a "convergence" that does not exist. See the ⚠ note below.

## The abusive build's combat score, by level (flat-7 unlock)

| Level | Raw budget | − baseline | For features | Rogue | Fighter | Barbarian | Fighter − Rogue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1  | 79  | 0   | 79  | 26  | 37  | 33  | **11** |
| 5  | 175 | 16  | 159 | 53  | 64  | 60  | **11** |
| 11 | 319 | 56  | 263 | 83  | 95  | 92  | **12** |
| 17 | 463 | 130 | 333 | 103 | 114 | 110 | **11** |
| 20 | 535 | 162 | 373 | 111 | 123 | 120 | **12** |

**Origin class is worth a flat 11–12 points at every level from 1 to 20.** It does not converge. (As a
share of the total it narrows — 30% at level 1, 10% at level 20 — but the absolute advantage is
constant.) Nobody reaches the pool maximum of 139 at any level.

## How much the unlock model actually moves it

Spread across all five candidate models at the same level and origin:

| Level | 1 | 5 | 11 | 17 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Max spread (points) | 1 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 4 |

**The unlock model is worth 1–4 points. Origin class is worth 11–12.** The model matters slightly more
at high level, because more unlocks become affordable, but never approaches the origin effect.

## The finding that cuts against flattening

Cheaper unlocks let the optimiser buy *more*, so flattening the ladder raises the abusive ceiling:

| Level 17, Rogue | current 7N | flat 10 | flat 7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Score | **99** | 102 | 103 |

The current escalating ladder is the most restrictive model tested — it holds the abusive build 4 points
lower than flat 7 does. Small, but it points the opposite way to the "7N is too punitive" argument, and
it puts flat 10 ahead of flat 7 on this axis where the break-even argument put flat 7 ahead. Both effects
are within a few points; neither is decisive on its own.

## HD-tied pricing: the optimiser defeats it

Every run under every HD-tied variant chose to unlock **at level 1**, where the character's tier — and
therefore the price — is lowest. Not one run chose to unlock late. An HD-tied unlock therefore collapses
to "flat, plus a bit" against anyone playing well, and the timing premium lands only on the player who
did not know to buy early. It taxes ignorance, not abuse.

This could not have been found by the static simulator; it needed
`testing/scripts/sim-unlock-timeline.mjs`, which replays a purchase timeline so unlock cost can be
stamped at the tier it was bought at.

## What "for features" still leaves out

The net budgets above deduct Hit Dice and proficiency only. Ordinary non-combat spending at level 20,
priced on the live engine (Fighter origin, marginal cost of each):

| | AP |
|---|---|
| Ability scores (STR/CON 20, DEX 16) | 96 |
| 2 saves | 13 |
| 6 skills | 6 |
| All armour + all weapons | 23 |
| 4 weapon masteries | 23 |
| **Typical total** | **≈161** |
| *(Vigor 5 / Grit 5, if taken)* | *160* |

So a realistic level-20 martial has around **212 AP** for combat features, not 373. Re-run any figure
above with `--reserve=161` for that view. Every score in this document is an upper bound.

## ⚠ Two withdrawn claims

Recorded because they were published before being checked, and both were used to argue a direction.

1. **"All three classes buy the entire combat pool with 40–90 AP spare."** False. The pool costs a Rogue
   491 AP against 373 available — 118 AP *over*.
2. **"Origin class stops mattering entirely by level 17."** False, and it was the bug's signature:
   inflating high-level budgets pushed every class to the pool ceiling. Corrected, the gap is constant.

Both came from comparing feature spend against the raw budget. Fixed in `f56436c`; the commit message
on `e7174bc` still states them and should not be cited.

## Separate defect found on the way

Six classes each sell a feature called "Extra Attack" and they are the same second attack. The engine
charges for all six — **102 AP for a Rogue** — and only warns that "the duplicates add no benefit".
A player can spend a fifth of a level-20 budget on nothing and be told so in a warning line.
